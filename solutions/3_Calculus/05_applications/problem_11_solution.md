# Solution to Problem 11: Volume of a Solid of Revolution

## 1. Problem Statement

The planar region bounded by the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$, the horizontal $x$-axis, and the vertical line $x = 4$ is revolved $360^\circ$ ($2\pi$ radians) about the $x$-axis to generate a solid of revolution (a circular paraboloid).

1. Construct the definite integral for the volume using the **disk method** from first geometric principles.
2. Compute the exact volume of the resulting solid.
3. Explain the geometric and physical origin of the factor $\pi y^2$ in the integrand.
4. Verify the result using Archimedes' classical formula for the volume of a paraboloid of revolution.

---

## 2. Theoretical Background and Concepts

1. **The Disk Method for Solids of Revolution:**
   Let a region bounded by $y = f(x) \ge 0$, the $x$-axis, and vertical lines $x = a$ and $x = b$ be revolved about the $x$-axis.
   - Slicing perpendicular to the rotation axis at coordinate $x$ creates a circular cross-section.
   - The radius of the cross-section is the vertical distance from the axis of rotation to the curve: $R(x) = f(x) = y$.
   - The area of this circular cross-section is:
     $$
     A(x) = \pi [R(x)]^2 = \pi [f(x)]^2 = \pi y^2
     $$
   - An infinitesimal slice of thickness $dx$ forms a thin circular cylinder (a "disk") with differential volume:
     $$
     dV = A(x) \, dx = \pi [f(x)]^2 \, dx
     $$
   - Summing all disks across the interval $[a, b]$ gives the total volume:
     $$
     V = \int_a^b \pi [f(x)]^2 \, dx = \pi \int_a^b y^2 \, dx
     $$

2. **Cavalieri's Principle:**
   The volume of any 3D body can be determined by integrating its cross-sectional area $A(x)$ along the perpendicular axis:
   $$
   V = \int_a^b A(x) \, dx
   $$

---

## 3. Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Construct the Volume Integral via the Disk Method

1. The boundary curve is:
   $$
   y = \sqrt{x}, \qquad x \in [0, 4]
   $$
2. The radius of the circular disk at position $x$ is:
   $$
   R(x) = y = \sqrt{x}
   $$
3. The cross-sectional area of the disk at position $x$ is:
   $$
   A(x) = \pi [R(x)]^2 = \pi (\sqrt{x})^2 = \pi x
   $$
4. The differential volume element of thickness $dx$ is:
   $$
   dV = A(x) \, dx = \pi x \, dx
   $$
5. Integrating from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$:
   $$
   V = \int_0^4 \pi (\sqrt{x})^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^4 x \, dx
   $$

---

### Step 2: Compute the Exact Volume

Evaluate the definite integral:
$$
V = \pi \int_0^4 x \, dx
$$

1. Find the antiderivative of $x$:
   $$
   \int x \, dx = \frac{x^2}{2}
   $$

2. Apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:
   $$
   V = \pi \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4
   $$

3. Evaluate at the boundaries:
   $$
   V = \pi \left(\frac{4^2}{2} - \frac{0^2}{2}\right) = \pi \left(\frac{16}{2} - 0\right) = \pi (8) = 8\pi
   $$

The exact volume of the solid of revolution is:
$$
\mathbf{V = 8\pi \approx 25.1327 \text{ cubic units}}
$$

---

### Step 3: Explanation of the Origin of the Factor $\pi y^2$

```text
       y
       ^                                    * (4, 2)
       |                                *   |
   y   |                * * * * * * * *     | Radius R = y = √x
       |        * * *                       |
       |  * *                               |
-------*------------------------------------+---------> x
       0                  x                 4
                          |<-- dx -->|
                          Thin Disk: dV = (π y^2) dx
```

1. **Circular Sweep of the Line Segment:**
   At any fixed coordinate $x \in [0, 4]$, consider the vertical line segment connecting the rotation axis $(x, 0)$ to the curve $(x, y)$.
   The length of this segment is $y = \sqrt{x}$.
   When this line segment revolves $360^\circ$ about its fixed anchor at $(x, 0)$, its outer tip traces out a circle of radius $r = y$.
   The segment itself sweeps out a complete, flat circular disc perpendicular to the $x$-axis.

2. **Euclidean Area of a Circle:**
   The area of a circle of radius $r$ is given by the ancient geometric formula:
   $$
   \text{Area} = \pi r^2
   $$
   Setting $r = y$ gives the cross-sectional area:
   $$
   A(x) = \pi y^2
   $$

3. **Cylindrical Disk Volume:**
   Multiplying this cross-sectional base area by the infinitesimal thickness $dx$ gives the volume of a right circular cylinder of radius $y$ and height $dx$:
   $$
   \text{Volume} = \text{Base Area} \times \text{Height} = (\pi y^2) \cdot dx
   $$
   The factor $\pi y^2$ is not an arbitrary formula; it is the exact planar area of the circular face of each infinitesimal slice.

---

## 4. Verification and Consistency Checks

1. **Verification via Archimedes' Formula for a Paraboloid:**
   The surface generated by rotating the parabola $x = y^2$ about its axis is a circular paraboloid.
   Archimedes proved in *On Conoids and Spheroids* (Proposition 21) that the volume of a segment of a circular paraboloid is exactly **half the volume of the circumscribed cylinder**:
   $$
   V_{\text{paraboloid}} = \frac{1}{2} V_{\text{cylinder}} = \frac{1}{2} (\pi R^2 H)
   $$
   For our solid:
   - Height (axial length): $H = 4$
   - Base radius at $x = 4$: $R = y(4) = \sqrt{4} = 2$
   - Volume of the circumscribed cylinder:
     $$
     V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi R^2 H = \pi (2^2)(4) = \pi (4)(4) = 16\pi
     $$
   Applying Archimedes' theorem:
   $$
   V = \frac{1}{2} (16\pi) = 8\pi \quad (\checkmark)
   $$
   Archimedes' classical geometric theorem confirms the calculus result.

2. **Alternative Verification via the Cylindrical Shell Method:**
   Re-compute the volume by slicing horizontally into cylindrical shells parallel to the rotation axis:
   - Variable of integration: $y \in [0, 2]$.
   - Radius of shell: $y$.
   - Height of shell: from $x = y^2$ to $x = 4$, so $h(y) = 4 - y^2$.
   - Shell volume formula:
     $$
     V = 2\pi \int_0^2 (\text{radius})(\text{height}) \, dy = 2\pi \int_0^2 y (4 - y^2) \, dy = 2\pi \int_0^2 (4y - y^3) \, dy
     $$
   - Evaluate the integral:
     $$
     V = 2\pi \left[2y^2 - \frac{y^4}{4}\right]_0^2 = 2\pi \left(2(2^2) - \frac{2^4}{4}\right) = 2\pi \left(8 - 4\right) = 2\pi (4) = 8\pi \quad (\checkmark)
     $$
   Both the disk method and the shell method produce identical volumes ($8\pi$).
