# Solution to Exercise 8: Intersection Point of a Line and a Plane

## 1. Problem Statement

Given the line $L$ and the plane $\Pi$ in three-dimensional space $\mathbb{R}^3$:
$$
L: (x, y, z) = (1, 0, 2) + t(1, 2, -1), \quad t \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
\Pi: x + y + z = 4
$$

1. Find the coordinates of the unique point of intersection $M = L \cap \Pi$.
2. Check the result by substituting the coordinates of point $M$ into both the parametric description of line $L$ and the Cartesian equation of plane $\Pi$.

---

## 2. Theoretical Background and Concepts

1. **Intersection of a Line and a Plane:**
   A point $M(x, y, z)$ lies simultaneously on line $L$ and plane $\Pi$ if:
   - Its coordinates can be generated by line $L$ for some value of parameter $t$, and
   - The coordinates satisfy the linear constraint of plane $\Pi$.

2. **Reduction to a Single Linear Equation:**
   Expressing the coordinates of $L$ parametrically:
   $$
   \begin{cases}
   x(t) = x_0 + t v_x \\
   y(t) = y_0 + t v_y \\
   z(t) = z_0 + t v_z
   \end{cases}
   $$
   and substituting into the plane equation $Ax + By + Cz = d$ gives:
   $$
   A(x_0 + t v_x) + B(y_0 + t v_y) + C(z_0 + t v_z) = d
   $$
   Rearranging terms:
   $$
   (A x_0 + B y_0 + C z_0) + t(A v_x + B v_y + C v_z) = d
   $$
   If $n \cdot v = A v_x + B v_y + C v_z \ne 0$, solving for $t$ yields the unique parameter value $t^*$ at the intersection point.

---

## 3. Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Write the Parametric Equations of the Line

From $L: (x, y, z) = (1, 0, 2) + t(1, 2, -1)$, write the coordinates in component form:
$$
\begin{cases}
x = 1 + t, \\
y = 0 + 2t = 2t, \\
z = 2 - t.
\end{cases}
$$

---

### Step 2: Substitute Parametric Expressions into the Plane Equation

Substitute $x, y, z$ into the equation of plane $\Pi: x + y + z = 4$:
$$
(1 + t) + (2t) + (2 - t) = 4
$$

Combine like terms:
- Linear terms in $t$: $t + 2t - t = 2t$
- Constant terms: $1 + 2 = 3$

The equation becomes:
$$
3 + 2t = 4
$$

---

### Step 3: Solve for the Parameter $t$

Isolate $t$:
$$
2t = 4 - 3 \implies 2t = 1 \implies t = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5
$$

---

### Step 4: Compute the Coordinates of the Intersection Point $M$

Substitute $t = \frac{1}{2}$ back into the parametric equations of line $L$:
- $x$-coordinate:
  $$
  x = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5
  $$
- $y$-coordinate:
  $$
  y = 2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 1
  $$
- $z$-coordinate:
  $$
  z = 2 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5
  $$

Thus, the point of intersection is:
$$
M = \left(\frac{3}{2}, 1, \frac{3}{2}\right) = (1.5, 1, 1.5)
$$

---

## 4. Verification and Consistency Checks

1. **Verification on Line $L$:**
   Check whether $M\left(\frac{3}{2}, 1, \frac{3}{2}\right)$ corresponds to a valid parameter value on $L$:
   $$
   \begin{pmatrix} 1.5 \\ 1.0 \\ 1.5 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.5 \\ 1.0 \\ -0.5 \end{pmatrix} = 0.5 \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{2} v \quad (\checkmark)
   $$
   Point $M$ lies on line $L$ for $t = \frac{1}{2}$.

2. **Verification on Plane $\Pi$:**
   Substitute the coordinates of $M$ into $x + y + z = 4$:
   $$
   \text{LHS} = \frac{3}{2} + 1 + \frac{3}{2} = \left(\frac{3}{2} + \frac{3}{2}\right) + 1 = 3 + 1 = 4
   $$
   $$
   \text{RHS} = 4 \quad (\checkmark)
   $$
   Since $\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}$, point $M$ strictly lies on plane $\Pi$.

3. **Transversality Check:**
   Compute the dot product of the line's direction $v = (1, 2, -1)$ and the plane's normal $n = (1, 1, 1)$:
   $$
   n \cdot v = (1)(1) + (1)(2) + (1)(-1) = 1 + 2 - 1 = 2 \ne 0
   $$
   Because $n \cdot v = 2 \ne 0$, the line is transverse to the plane, ensuring that the intersection point is unique.
