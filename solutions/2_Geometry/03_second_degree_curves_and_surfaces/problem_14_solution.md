# Solution to Exercise 14: Cone and Cylinder

## 1. Problem Statement

Compare the two second-degree surfaces of revolution in $\mathbb{R}^3$:
$$
\text{Surface 1: } x^2 + y^2 = z^2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \text{Surface 2: } x^2 + y^2 = 4
$$

1. For each surface, describe and classify the planar cross-sections obtained by slicing with:
   - Horizontal planes $z = c$ (for constant $c \in \mathbb{R}$),
   - The vertical coordinate plane $x = 0$,
   - The vertical coordinate plane $y = 0$.
2. State the standard geometric name of each surface.
3. Explain why the radius of the horizontal cross-section depends on $z$ in the first surface, but is strictly constant in the second surface.

---

## 2. Theoretical Background and Concepts

1. **Circular Cones:**
   An equation of the form $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = z^2$ represents a cone with apex at the origin.
   - It is a homogeneous quadratic surface: if $(x, y, z)$ is on the cone, then for every scalar $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$, $(\lambda x, \lambda y, \lambda z)$ is also on the cone.
   - The surface is formed by straight ruling lines passing through the origin.

2. **Cylinders in $\mathbb{R}^3$:**
   In three-dimensional space, an equation in which one variable is completely absent represents a **cylinder**:
   - If the equation $F(x, y) = 0$ does not contain the variable $z$, the curve $F(x, y) = 0$ in the $xy$-plane serves as the directrix.
   - The surface is generated by moving straight lines (generators) parallel to the absent coordinate axis ($z$-axis) through every point of the directrix curve.

---

## 3. Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Cross-Sectional Analysis of Surface 1 ($x^2 + y^2 = z^2$)

1. **Horizontal Cross-Sections $z = c$:**
   Substitute $z = c$ into the equation:
   $$
   x^2 + y^2 = c^2
   $$
   - **For $c \ne 0$:**
     $x^2 + y^2 = (|c|)^2$ is a **circle** in the plane $z = c$ centered on the $z$-axis at $(0, 0, c)$ with radius:
     $$
     R_{\text{cone}}(c) = |c|
     $$
     The radius grows linearly with the absolute distance $|c|$ from the $xy$-plane.
   - **For $c = 0$:**
     $x^2 + y^2 = 0 \implies x = 0, y = 0$. The cross-section is the **single point** $(0, 0, 0)$ (the apex of the cone).

2. **Cross-Section by $x = 0$ (the $yz$-plane):**
   Substitute $x = 0$:
   $$
   0^2 + y^2 = z^2 \iff z^2 - y^2 = 0 \iff (z - y)(z + y) = 0
   $$
   This represents a **pair of straight lines** intersecting at the origin:
   $$
   z = y \quad \text{and} \quad z = -y
   $$
   making angles of $45^\circ$ with the coordinate axes.

3. **Cross-Section by $y = 0$ (the $xz$-plane):**
   Substitute $y = 0$:
   $$
   x^2 + 0^2 = z^2 \iff z^2 - x^2 = 0 \iff (z - x)(z + x) = 0
   $$
   This represents a **pair of straight lines** intersecting at the origin:
   $$
   z = x \quad \text{and} \quad z = -x
   $$

**Geometric Identification:**
Surface 1 is a **right circular double cone** (Polish: *stożek kołowy*) with vertex at the origin $(0, 0, 0)$ and vertical axis of symmetry $Oz$.

---

### Step 2: Cross-Sectional Analysis of Surface 2 ($x^2 + y^2 = 4$)

1. **Horizontal Cross-Sections $z = c$:**
   Substitute $z = c$ into the equation:
   $$
   x^2 + y^2 = 4 = 2^2
   $$
   For **every** value of $c \in \mathbb{R}$, this defines a **circle** of constant radius:
   $$
   R_{\text{cyl}} = 2
   $$
   centered at $(0, 0, c)$. The cross-section is identical at all heights.

2. **Cross-Section by $x = 0$ (the $yz$-plane):**
   Substitute $x = 0$:
   $$
   0^2 + y^2 = 4 \implies y^2 = 4 \implies y = \pm 2
   $$
   In the $yz$-plane, this represents a **pair of parallel vertical straight lines**:
   $$
   y = 2 \quad \text{and} \quad y = -2
   $$

3. **Cross-Section by $y = 0$ (the $xz$-plane):**
   Substitute $y = 0$:
   $$
   x^2 + 0^2 = 4 \implies x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2
   $$
   In the $xz$-plane, this represents a **pair of parallel vertical straight lines**:
   $$
   x = 2 \quad \text{and} \quad x = -2
   $$

**Geometric Identification:**
Surface 2 is a **right circular cylinder** (Polish: *walec kołowy*) of radius $R = 2$ whose longitudinal axis of symmetry is the $z$-axis.

---

### Step 3: Why Radius Depends on $z$ in the Cone but is Constant in the Cylinder

1. **Explicit Algebraic Presence vs. Absence of Variable $z$:**
   - In the cone equation $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$, the height coordinate $z$ appears explicitly as a quadratic term. The equation couples horizontal position $(x, y)$ directly with vertical height $z$. The radius is explicitly a function of height: $R(z) = |z|$.
   - In the cylinder equation $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, the variable $z$ does not appear in the formula at all ($\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(x^2 + y^2 - 4) = 0$).

2. **Geometric Consequences of a Free Variable:**
   - Because $z$ is unconstrained, if a 2D point $(x_0, y_0)$ satisfies $x_0^2 + y_0^2 = 4$, then the point $(x_0, y_0, z)$ satisfies the equation for **all possible real values of $z \in (-\infty, \infty)$**.
   - Geometrically, this sweeps out a vertical straight line parallel to the $z$-axis passing through $(x_0, y_0, 0)$.
   - The cylinder is formed by an infinite bundle of parallel vertical lines, guaranteeing that every cross-section perpendicular to these generators has the identical base circle radius of $2$.
   - In contrast, the ruling lines of the cone are not parallel; they all pass through the common origin $(0, 0, 0)$ at an inclination of $45^\circ$, causing the horizontal circular cross-section to flare out proportionally to $|z|$.

---

## 4. Verification and Consistency Checks

1. **Comparative Overview Table:**

| Feature | Circular Cone ($x^2 + y^2 = z^2$) | Circular Cylinder ($x^2 + y^2 = 4$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Slice $z = c$** | Circle with radius $R(c) = \|c\|$ | Circle with constant radius $R = 2$ |
| **Slice $z = 0$** | Apex point $(0, 0, 0)$ | Circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ |
| **Slice $x = 0$** | Intersecting lines $z = \pm y$ | Parallel lines $y = \pm 2$ |
| **Slice $y = 0$** | Intersecting lines $z = \pm x$ | Parallel lines $x = \pm 2$ |
| **Homogeneity** | Homogeneous of degree 2 | Inhomogeneous |
| **Generators** | Lines passing through apex $(0, 0, 0)$ | Parallel lines directed along vector $(0, 0, 1)$ |

2. **Homogeneity Scaling Check on Cone:**
   Let $P = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$ satisfy $x_0^2 + y_0^2 = z_0^2$.
   Test scaled point $\lambda P = (\lambda x_0, \lambda y_0, \lambda z_0)$:
   $$
   (\lambda x_0)^2 + (\lambda y_0)^2 = \lambda^2 (x_0^2 + y_0^2) = \lambda^2 z_0^2 = (\lambda z_0)^2 \quad (\checkmark)
   $$
   The entire ray through $P$ belongs to the surface, validating the cone structure.
